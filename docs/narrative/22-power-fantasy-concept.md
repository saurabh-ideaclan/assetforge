# 22. Power Fantasy pilot: story concept (revised after review; for the owner's approval)

Title: **Null** (subtitle *The Weight He Carries*; the earlier working title). Names are global and invented, none tied to a real country (the owner's note of 2026-11-11: no Korean names for a global audience). Status: concept v2, canon
only once approved. Genre: power fantasy / progression / awakening, family drama, conspiracy. Global readers,
simple natural English, manhwa-inspired art. Phase 4 of the brief. The independent review is
[reviews/opus-review-power-fantasy-concept.md](reviews/opus-review-power-fantasy-concept.md); §11 lists what it
changed.

## 1. Premise in three sentences

In the port city of Calder, power is a loan: the awakened borrow strength from the Undertow and pay for it in
pieces of themselves, and the city is ranked by how much each person is allowed to borrow. Eli Hale, nineteen, is
registered as a Null, the son of a disgraced assessor serving twelve years for falsifying the readings that decide
everyone's future; his mother Mara keeps the family's repair shop and its debts alive by force of will. When a
Seam opens under the salvage yard where Eli works illegally, he discovers he is not empty but *full*: he was born
carrying someone else's debt, and he can carry more, which makes him the most valuable and most dangerous thing in
a city whose real economy is other people paying your price.

## 2. The world (original; no gates, letter ranks, system windows or shadow armies)

- **The Undertow.** Something under the world that lends. Nobody knows what it is; theories are a religion and a
  science. Where it presses close, **Seams** open: tears in ordinary places (a car park, a school pool) where
  gravity, light and sound go wrong and **Drift** comes through: things shaped from what the Seam swallowed
  (a staircase that walks, a dog made of rain, a crowd of coats). Drift is dangerous and strange, not demonic.
- **The Ledger.** Every person has one, visible only to assessors through a **Reading**. It records what you have
  borrowed and what you owe. Awakening means the Undertow accepted your first loan. Power use costs **Toll**:
  memories, years, warmth, sleep, a sense, a name. Small uses cost small things; a desperate use can cost a
  childhood. This is the rule the whole world lives by, and the one the criminals bend.
- **Measure.** The Registry rates how much a person may borrow, 1 to 10. A **Null** has no Measure: the Undertow
  will not lend to them. Measure decides schooling, licences, insurance, marriage prospects. It is caste by
  bureaucracy, believed fixed at eighteen.
- **Wardens.** Licensed awakened who seal Seams and clear Drift, organised in **Firms** that hold territory and
  contracts with the city. Firms are companies with crews, not guilds. A Firm's reputation is its Measure
  average, so Firms hoard high-Measure people and will not touch a Null.
- **The Tithe.** The criminal organisation. Its business is **transfer**: moving Toll from one Ledger to another.
  It sells awakening to people the Registry refused, lends power to the rich at no cost to them, and makes someone
  else pay: **Bearers**, people whose Ledgers can hold other people's Toll. Bearers are kept, used up and
  replaced. Nobody official admits Bearers exist, because the Registry's own **Settlement** facilities do the same
  thing to convicts, legally: twelve years of Settlement pays the city's Toll for sealing Seams and takes most of a
  life's warmth. Eli's father is there.
- **Scale.** The Tithe has hundreds of clients and dozens of Bearers; the Registry audits thousands of readings a
  year. The Hales are one small account in a large book. Only two of the city's secrets touch them directly
  (§4, §11), and the rest of the cast have their own.

## 3. The power system: Bearing, Weight, Letting Go, Calling

One rule, read four ways. Written so a writer cannot trip on it.

1. **Bearing (first awakening, chapter 2).** When an awakened person near Eli uses power, Eli can choose to pay
   their Toll instead of them. For them the use is free, which for a Warden in a Seam is the difference between
   sealing it and dying in it. He cannot borrow for himself; the Registry's reading was *technically* right.
2. **Weight (one definition).** Toll that Eli has paid for someone else stays on his Ledger as **Weight**. It is
   both a *claim* (the Undertow owes it back) and a *load* (the body strains to hold it: grey in the eyes, cold
   hands, a day he can no longer remember, then worse). Capacity is how much Weight he can hold; it grows with use,
   slowly. Over capacity is **Foreclosure**: the body pays everything at once. So "carry everything" is never the
   best play: every carry spends capacity he may need for the person who matters next.
3. **Letting go (the pilot's visible edge, learned in chapter 3).** A carry can be dropped mid-use. The Toll then
   lands on the user at once, in full. Against a Tithe collector burning borrowed power, this is a weapon: Eli
   makes him pay his own bill, on the spot, in public. It is the first thing anyone *sees* Eli do, and it is how
   the city will later learn to fear him. It is also ugly, and the story treats it as such.
4. **Calling (second awakening, chapter 7).** The Ledger runs both ways. Eli can **call in** Weight: spend a
   stored claim to manifest an echo of the power whose price he paid (he paid for a Warden's wall of stopped air;
   he can raise that wall once, and then that Weight is gone). Rules: only what he has carried; each call spends
   it; the call costs him a small Toll of his own (the Undertow takes interest, so calling is never free relief);
   and the call brings the lender's memory of the moment with it, so every power he uses teaches him something
   intimate about the person it came from, and leaves him with their loss for a while. Echoes are never figures;
   they are the thing the power did. Preparation: the reading ("full, not empty") in chapter 3; Idris's line in
   chapter 2 ("the Ledger is a door, not a wall"); a Bearing upgrade in chapters 4–5 (carrying for two at once,
   choosing what to pay with); the involuntary call in chapter 5 that he does not understand.
5. **Progression after the pilot.** Capacity; choosing which memory to spend; carrying for several; the question
   the season ends on: whether to call in the debt he was born with, which would free him and pay it to whoever
   the Tithe's client was.

Red lines kept: no interface, quests, levels, shadows, secret dungeon, "sole player"; no re-measurement day.

## 4. The family, with the moral complexity the brief asks for

- **Tomas Hale (father, 52).** A Registry assessor. His small, real mistake, eleven years ago: he nudged a
  stranger's reading from 2 to 3 for a bribe he told himself was a kindness (the boy kept a scholarship; the boy
  is nobody in this story). The Tithe keeps assessors' secrets the way banks keep deeds. From then on he was
  asked, then told, to misread: to mark the Tithe's unlicensed awakened as Nulls so the Registry would never look
  for them, and to mark their Bearers as Nulls so nobody would ask why these people were ill. He took money for
  it, for years; the shop was bought with some of it. When the audit came he confessed to the readings and nothing
  else, and took the twelve years. Two truths unfold slowly. First: at eighteen he read his own son as Null,
  deliberately, which kept the Tithe from learning what Eli was *and* kept his own network from being exposed
  through his son; protection and self-protection in one act, decided without asking the boy whose future it
  closed. Second: the debt Eli was born with is a transfer Tomas performed, under orders, for a Tithe client he
  never met; he knows the account number, not the name, and he has spent twelve years not finding out. He is not
  innocent, not a monster, and not the centre of the world: he was useful, and then he was used up.
- **Mara Hale (mother, 48).** Runs *Hale Repair* (small machines, Seam-damaged goods nobody else will touch).
  Sharp, funny when tired, proud to a fault; she negotiates with collectors like a chess player and refuses
  charity that comes with a face attached. She knew the money was wrong before she knew where it came from, and
  she kept the shop; she has made her peace with that and will not apologise for it to her son. She visits Tomas
  every month and has not told Eli. Her decision in the pilot is on the page: in chapter 2 she refuses Selene's
  offer (the family's debt cleared if Eli "comes in for a reading"); in chapter 3 the bank calls the shop loan,
  and she takes her elder son Oren's money with its condition (the shop in his name) rather than the Tithe's.
  Her relationship with Eli is love expressed as management; the pilot's quietest, best scenes are the two of
  them doing accounts at the kitchen table.
- **Eli Hale (protagonist, 19).** Guarded, quick, proud in the way of people who have been looked down on; he
  keeps a ledger of slights in his head and calls it realism. Kind in actions, sharp in words. Flaws that make
  scenes: he decides for other people "for their own good" (his father's flaw, which he would hate to hear); he
  takes the hit rather than ask; he lies to his mother to protect her. He wants a licence, money for the shop,
  and for one person at a Firm to look at him twice. His power is his father's trade turned inside out: carrying
  other people's debt. Voice: short sentences, dry, counts things.

## 6. Player choices and consequences

Six persistent stats, each read by later scenes: `weight` (resource 0–10: carried Toll; high weight shows on him
and closes options), `capacity` (resource: grows, caps weight), `standing` (world: how the city sees him; moves
slowly and lags capability on purpose), `trust_mara`, `trust_idris` (relationship), `father` (personality axis
−3 refuses him … +3 seeks him). Who knows what he can do is tracked per faction as flags (`known_idris`,
`known_firm`, `known_tithe`, `known_registry`), plus decision flags (`took_job`, `told_mara`, `took_card`,
`saved_penny`, `let_go_in_public`, `cato_helped`). Nothing else; the stat design is small enough to remember.

Decision families, each with observable consequences:

- **Humiliation.** At the Firm's open trials Eli works the gate, checking Measure cards; Cato makes him read his
  own card aloud. Swallow it (standing kept; Noor sees him swallow it, and ch3 remembers), answer it (a scuffle
  he loses; Cato remembers; the Firm bars him from the yard, which changes where he is when the Seam opens), or
  walk off the job (Mara's month, and Oren's letter gets teeth).
- **Reveal or conceal.** Carrying for Idris in the Seam is involuntary; telling him afterwards is a choice.
  Revealed: training, a crew place by ch3, `known_idris`, and Idris's own secret in return. Concealed: safety, a
  slower climb, and Idris finds out in ch4 with trust lost.
- **Money and risk.** The salvage yard pays Nulls to go where Wardens will not. Take the night job (debt down,
  Eli is in the pit with Idris when the Seam opens) or refuse (he comes back at dusk for his last wages and is at
  the fence when Idris's whistle sounds inside; he goes in because nobody else is left). Either way he is there;
  the choice decides whether he is paid to be, and whether the boss has footage to sell.
- **What to lose.** Carrying costs a memory. In ch3 the player chooses which, from three planted in ch1–2 (the
  radio his father taught him to fix; the morning Oren left; the first time Noor laughed at one of his jokes).
  The lost one is gone: later scenes that would call on it play differently, and Eli does not know what he lost.
- **The father.** Visit, refuse, or investigate without him; each gives different pieces of the truth and a
  different Tomas when they finally talk.
- **The Tithe.** Selene's card: keep it, burn it, or use it once. Using it saves Penny faster in ch3 and marks
  Eli's Ledger in a way Pike can read.
- **Power's use.** Whose cost to take when two people need it; whether to let go in public (the rescue seen by
  the street: standing up, `known_firm`, and a line Eli cannot walk back); later, whether to carry for Cato.

Branch-and-converge: chapters converge on the same places and antagonists; the flags decide who is in the room,
who trusts him, and which version of a scene plays (the ch3 rescue has three shapes).

Pacing discipline: hardship through events in ch1 (not told), hope by the end of ch2, a real win with a real
price by ch3, something seen by ch3, a Bearing upgrade before the second awakening, Calling at ch7. The
protagonist is never overpowered: Calling spends what Bearing stored and costs interest, so every display of
strength is a choice against future safety.

## 8. Chapter 1–3 scene design (pilot)

Each chapter: 1,500 words, 6–7 choices, first choice by box 8, one callback from ch3, two or three places, at
most one music change, ambience per place, sfx only for shown events (the Seam opening, a shutter, the
laundrette's dryers). Planted memories are written as small warm beats in ch1–2, so losing one in ch3 hurts.

- **Ch1 Null.** (a) The Firm's yard gate, trials morning: Measure cards, Cato, Noor; choice: humiliation. (b) The
  salvage yard, afternoon: the boss, the night job; choice: money. Planted memory: the radio. (c) Hale Repair,
  evening: Mara, the accounts, Oren's letter; choice: tell her about the job. Planted memory: Noor's laugh
  (she drops by). Dusk at the yard: the ground sings; the Seam; a Warden's whistle inside it. Music: none until
  dusk, then one low cue.
- **Ch2 The Seam.** (a) Inside: Idris, wounded, trying to seal it; Drift. The involuntary carry: Eli pays, Idris
  seals; Eli loses a morning he will never remember (planted memory: the morning Oren left, told here as a
  flashback so the player holds it). (b) Out, dawn: Idris's question; choice: reveal or conceal. (c) Hale Repair:
  Selene's visit, Mara's no on the page; choice: the card. Consequences of ch1: the boss's footage; whether Mara
  was at the fence; whether the Firm has barred him.
- **Ch3 What He Carries.** (a) Idris's licensing office: what a Bearer is; the reading ("full, not empty");
  letting go, taught on a candle. (b) The laundrette: Penny; Selene's collector burns borrowed power; choice: carry
  for Penny, or let go and make the collector pay in front of the street; choice: which memory. (c) Hale Repair,
  night: the bank's letter; Oren's money and its condition; Mara's visiting pass found; choice: the father.
  Payoff: Idris's offer, the first thing Eli has ever been chosen for; or the secret kept and the offer withheld.
  Hook: Idris, reading Eli's old Registry printout, goes quiet at the assessor's initials and will not say why.

## 9. Originality check against the reference

Shared with the genre, deliberately: awakening as social mobility, a ranked society, an underdog who hides growing
power, a second awakening as the turning point, organisations that hoard the strong. Not shared: the loan/toll
economy, Bearers and the Settlement parallel, letting go, Calling, the assessor father, a protagonist whose
strength begins as paying for others, Seams and Drift instead of gates and monsters, no interface or levels, no
re-measurement day. Echoes are never drawn as figures.

## 10. What the owner decides now

1. The title *Null* and the names (Eli, Mara, Tomas, Idris, Noor, Cato, Selene, Pike, Oren, Penny): global, invented, none tied to a country.
2. The power system as written (Bearing → letting go → Calling, with Weight as both claim and load).
3. The father's shape: assessor, the bribe, the money, the Null reading that protected them both.
4. Twelve chapters with Calling at 7, or a shorter season.
5. The pilot runs in guided mode with approvals at cast and outline (set).

## 11. What the review changed (v1 → v2)

Weight is one thing (claim and load), Calling costs interest, so carrying everything is never the best play.
Letting go gives the pilot a power someone *sees*. Cato is no longer the nudged boy; his power is paid by Penny,
which ties him to the Tithe's business rather than to the Hales, and makes the ch3 rescue reach him without
coincidence. Selene did not put the debt on Eli; the client is unknown even to Tomas, and the Hales are one
account among many. The night-job branch puts Eli at the yard either way. The retest day and the retest-paper
hook are gone (the open trials and Idris's silence replace them). Settlement is the prison only; the body paying
all at once is Foreclosure. The memory cost is a choice among planted memories that later scenes respect. Tomas's
Null reading protected himself too; Mara knew the money was wrong and kept the shop, and her no has a visible
price. Calling moves to chapter 7 with a Bearing upgrade at 4–5. Stats cut to six plus per-faction knowledge.
Chapters have two or three places, not one.
